# Global Online Casino & Slots — Regional Markets and Regulation

**Research cut-off: 29 July 2026. Latest actuals are FY2025 / FY2024-25 unless stated.**

**Notation:** `[V]` = verified against a regulator, government or company primary source. `[E]` = estimate from an analyst, trade body or trade press; treat as directional. Where sources disagree it says so rather than picking one. Where something could not be verified it says that too — nothing below is invented.

This is the third of three research files behind the dashboard in `igaming-research/`. The other two are `slot-studio-competitive-landscape-2026.md` (supplier benchmarking) and `slot-mechanics-and-player-preferences-2026.md` (game mechanics and player behaviour).

## 1. Executive summary — the eight things that matter

1. **Online is about to become the majority of global gambling.** H2 Gambling Capital's April 2026 update puts global GGR at $712bn in 2024, crossing $1,031bn in 2030 and $1,088bn in 2031. Online grew from $89bn (20% of total) in 2015 to $293bn (41%) in 2024 and is forecast at $530bn (51%) in 2030 — passing 50% for the first time in 2029. Online casino is the faster-growing half: $233bn by 2030 versus $248bn for online betting, essentially level by 2031. `[V — H2GC]`
2. **The UK just imposed the most punitive online casino tax in a major market.** Remote Gaming Duty went from 21% to **40% on 1 April 2026**. UK online slots GGY was £4.2bn in FY2024/25 and is still growing — Q4 FY2025/26 slots GGY was £773m, up 12% year-on-year despite the £5/£2 stake caps. `[V — UKGC, HMRC]`
3. **Tax is now the biggest variable in market attractiveness, and the Netherlands is the cautionary tale.** Dutch gambling tax went 30.5% → 34.2% (2025) → 37.8% (2026), plus a 1.95% levy. Legal GGR fell ~18% and GGR-based channelisation dropped to **53%** — more money is now wagered with unlicensed operators than licensed ones. The €200m of extra revenue never appeared; 2025 tax take came in *below* 2024. `[V — Ksa]`
4. **Pragmatic Play is the world's largest slot supplier by share and is essentially absent from the United States regulated market.** Verified against Pennsylvania's own iGaming Manufacturers Eligibility List dated 28 July 2026 — Pragmatic Play and Arrise do not appear, while Games Global, Play'n GO, Playtech, Evolution US, Hacksaw, Relax and ~40 others do. NEXT.io reported the same in September 2025. This is the largest single addressable-market gap for any tier-one slot studio.
5. **Pragmatic Play has one confirmed regulatory penalty: CAD $40,000 from the AGCO on 7 May 2026** for allowing its games onto unregulated sites accessible to Ontario players. No UKGC action against Pragmatic Play was found — but the UKGC's £4.75m settlement with Evolution in July 2026 over exactly that conduct sets the benchmark the whole supply side is now measured against.
6. **US iGaming grew 27.6% to a record $10.74bn in 2025 but is still stuck in seven states.** Maine was the only legalisation in 2025; Maryland and Virginia both punted to 2027. Meanwhile 14 states have shut sweepstakes casinos, a ~$4bn parallel market. `[V — AGA]`
7. **Crypto casinos are the biggest unmeasured pool in the industry.** Stake alone did an estimated $4.7bn GGR in 2024 — comparable to Entain. Category-wide estimates of $81.4bn (2024) exist but have never been audited and should not be charted without a heavy caveat. `[E]`
8. **India deleted itself from the map.** The Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 took effect 1 May 2026 and bans all real-money online gaming outright with no licensing pathway. The domestic industry shrank from ~$6bn to ~$1bn.

### 3.2 United Kingdom — largest disclosed slot market in the world

**FY2024/25 (April 2024 – March 2025)** `[V]`
- Total industry GGY **£16.8bn** (+7.3%); Remote Casino, Betting and Bingo **£7.8bn** (+13.1%), 46% of market
- Online casino games **£5.0bn**, of which **online slots £4.2bn**
- Remote betting £2.6bn (football £1.3bn, horse racing £766.7m); remote bingo £165.6m
- Land-based £4.8bn (29%); lotteries £4.2bn (25%)

Source: https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/statistics-and-research/publication/industry-statistics-annual-report-financial-year-april-2024-to-march-2025

**Quarterly operator data (largest operators, ~70% of market)** `[V]`

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Monthly slots GGY, Q4: Jan 2026 £263.2m / Feb £247.1m / Mar £262.6m, versus Jan 2025 £226.0m / Feb £212.1m / Mar £250.5m.

Sources: https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/statistics-and-research/publication/market-overview-operator-data-to-march-2026-published-may-2026 · https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/statistics-and-research/publication/gambling-business-data-on-gambling-to-march-2026-published-may-2026

The pattern matters for anyone modelling the stake caps: **GGY per session and spins per session both fell, but total slot GGY rose 12%** because the active player base grew 6% and spin volume 7%. The caps compressed unit economics and were more than offset by volume.

**Tax** `[V]` — RGD **21% → 40%** for accounting periods beginning on or after **1 April 2026**. A new remote General Betting Duty of **25%** (from 15%) from **April 2027**, excluding spread betting, pool bets and horse racing. HMRC's own costing assumes operators pass on **up to 90%** of the increase via prices or reduced payouts.
Sources: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/changes-to-gambling-duties/gambling-duty-changes · https://igamingbusiness.com/finance/uk-sector-hit-with-remote-gaming-duty-increase/

**Product rules:** £5 max stake per spin for all adults from 9 April 2025; £2 for adults aged 18–24 from 21 May 2025. Mandatory deposit limits for first-time depositors from October 2025. Financial risk checks approved.

*Caveat:* pre-Budget trade press reported an expected Machine Games Duty rise from 20% to 50%; the final enacted rate was not verified and is excluded from the tables.

### 3.3 Germany — the most restrictive major regulated slot regime

**GGL Tätigkeitsbericht 2025, published 3 July 2026** `[V]`
- Total regulated market GGR **€14.4bn**, flat YoY; land-based €10.9bn; **online €3.5bn (+2%)**
- **Virtual slot machines: €543m (+11%, +€53m)** on stakes of **€4.6bn**
- Online poker €69m; sports betting €1.887bn (−4%) on €8.3bn stakes
- LUGAS registered players **5.3m**
- Enforcement 2025: 621 supervisory measures, 531 administrative procedures, 287 prohibition proceedings; **1,843 illegal websites no longer reachable from Germany**, 178 DNS-blocked

Sources: https://www.gluecksspiel-behoerde.de/de/news/ggl-veroeffentlicht-taetigkeitsbericht-2025 · https://glueckswirtschaft.de/hintergrund/ggl-taetigkeitsbericht-2025-mehr-aufsicht-mehr-druck-auf-illegales-gluecksspiel

**Channelisation:** the GGL declined to publish its own 2025 figure and relies on the Blockchain Research Lab study, which put the unlicensed share of online GGR at **22.97% for 2024 — a 77% channelisation rate**. Note the tension: the same report estimates black-market GGR at only €547m, which the GGL frames as ~4% of the entire licensed market including land-based. Those framings are not comparable. Use 77% and flag the methodology.

**Regime:** €1 maximum stake per spin, minimum 5-second spin duration, no autoplay, no jackpots, no bonus buys, cross-operator €1,000 monthly deposit limit via LUGAS, 5.3% tax **on stakes**. Live casino and online table games are not licensed federally — they sit with individual Länder, which is why Germany's online casino market is effectively slots-only. Because tax is on turnover at €1/spin, effective tax on GGR is roughly 40–55%, which is why Germany produces only €543m of slot GGR from a population of 84 million versus £4.2bn in the UK from 68 million.

**Product loosening, 1 July 2026:** the flat €1/spin limit was replaced by a tiered system — **€1 under 21, €3 for 21+, €5 for 21+ players showing no harm indicators over a 90-day qualification period**. First time the GGL has used its Interstate Treaty powers to loosen a slot parameter; the stated rationale is channelisation. Source: https://next.io/news/regulation/germany-quietly-raises-stake-limit-online-slots/

### 3.6 Sweden

**Spelinspektionen, full year 2025** `[V]` — commercial online gaming and betting net turnover **SEK 18,412m** (Q1 4,295 / Q2 4,632 / Q3 4,534 / Q4 4,951); state lottery and VLT SEK 5,528m; charity lotteries SEK 3,759m; total market SEK 28,196m.
Source: https://www.spelinspektionen.se/globalassets/dokument/statistik/spelmarknaden-2025.pdf

**Channelisation 2025: 84%**, down from 85% (2024) and 86% (2023), against a 90% target. The headline averages two divergent methods — a player survey giving **89%** and an internet-traffic estimate giving **78%**. By vertical: sports betting **96%**, online casino **81%** (traffic method: 95% betting, **68% casino**). Spelinspektionen identified **2,186 active unlicensed sites** as of 30 April 2026, of which **976 were online-casino-only**.

### 3.7 Netherlands — the clearest tax-elasticity case study in Europe

**Kansspelautoriteit monitoring, spring 2026** `[V]`

| Period | Online GGR | Change |
|---|---|---|
| H2 2024 | €697m | — |
| H1 2025 | €600m | −16% vs H2 2024 |
| H2 2025 | **€602m** | flat; **−18% vs 2024** |
| Q1 2026 | €321m | ~€100m/month run rate |

- **Channelisation by GGR: 53% in H2 2025** (51% at end-2024). Channelisation by *players* is 91%.
- Average monthly playing accounts rose to **1.38m** from 1.29m — the Ksa attributes this to players spreading across accounts to stay under the net-deposit threshold introduced in October 2024.
- Average monthly loss per player fell from **€146** (end-2024) to **€119** (early 2025).
- 2025 was the first year since the 2021 market opening in which **the illegal market exceeded the legal one**.

Sources: https://kansspelautoriteit.nl/monitoringsrapportage-voorjaar-2026-marktontwikkeling-blijft-gelijk · https://kansspelautoriteit.nl/sites/default/files/2026-06/Monitor%20effecten%20verhoging%20Kansspelbelasting%20-%20juni%202026.pdf

**Tax:** 30.5% (2024) → **34.2% (1 Jan 2025)** → **37.8% (1 Jan 2026)**, plus a 1.95% levy. The stated goal was ~€200m extra annual revenue from 2026; the Ksa's own research confirms 2025 gambling tax revenue came in **below** 2024.

**Critically for suppliers,** the Ksa's 2026 regulatory agenda **extends supervision to B2B suppliers, payment providers, hosting companies and social platforms** rather than pursuing operators alone. Source: https://affpapa.com/kansspelautoriteit-targets-b2b-suppliers-in-illegal-gambling/

### 3.8 Denmark — online casino overtakes lotteries for the first time

**Spillemyndigheden, *Spilmarkedet i tal 2025*** `[V]`
- Total GGR **DKK 11.5bn (−1%)**
- **Online casino DKK 4.31bn (38% of market), +12.1%, +139% since 2012** — now the largest single segment, passing lotteries (DKK 3.49bn)
- **Online slots: 82% of online casino GGR = DKK 3.54bn.** Roulette DKK 246m, blackjack DKK 267m
- Online = **73% of total GGR**; **69% of online casino revenue is mobile**
- **41 online casino licences** at end-2025 (of 1,970 licences in total)

Sources: https://igamingbusiness.com/finance/online-casinos-lotteries-largest-danish-gambling-segment-2025/ · https://www.pressgiochi.it/en/danimarca-nel-2025-il-ggr-complessivo-ha-raggiunto-115-mld-di-corone-danesi/151701

Methodology caveat: Spillemyndigheden restated GGR to 2026 price levels, so 2025 figures are **not directly comparable to previously published reports**. Denmark is proportionally the purest slot market in Europe — slots are 31% of the *entire* national gambling market.

### 3.9 France — online casino still banned

Online casino remains **prohibited**; the ANJ licenses sports betting, horse race betting and poker only.

- 2025 total French gambling GGR **€14.1bn (+3%)**; H1 2025 online €1.4bn (+6%) `[V via ANJ figures reported by EEGaming]`
- The 2026 Finance Act made Paris gaming clubs permanent but **did not address online casinos at all**. An amendment to legalise was introduced during the 2025 budget debates and dropped after land-based casino opposition. Nothing official is in progress.
- ANJ Chair Marie-Laure Denis estimates the **illegal market at €800m–€1.5bn GGR**, growing, **"mainly driven by illegal online casinos"**.
- ANJ Director-General Pauline Hot: if online casinos were authorised it would have to be "within a very strict regulatory framework, probably stricter than for other games, because the design of these games is particularly addictive."

Source: https://eegaming.org/latest-news/2026/03/02/134887/whats-up-and-whats-next-on-the-french-gambling-market/

France is the largest unserved online casino opportunity in Western Europe, and there is no live legislative vehicle.

### 3.10 Romania — the biggest tax shock in CEE

**Law 141/2025, in force 1 August 2025** `[V]`

| Item | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Annual authorisation tax (online) | 21% of GGR | **30% of GGR** |
| Minimum annual authorisation tax | €400,000 | **€480,000** |
| Annual licence fee | €15,600–€312,000 tiered | **Flat €300,000** |
| Class 2 (B2B supplier) licence | — | **€20,000/year** |
| Responsible gambling contribution | — | €500,000/year |

**Directly relevant to suppliers:** Law 141/2025 requires **software providers, platform providers and payment processors** to block any attempt to access their services from Romania via an unlicensed online operator. Romania has effectively legislated the AGCO/UKGC supply-chain doctrine.

Market structure: **~31–32 licensed online operators**, stable 2022–2025. Land-based slot machines fell from ~89,000 (2022) to **~45,000 (2025)**.
Sources: https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/gaming-law-2025/romania/trends-and-developments · https://evz.ro/exclusiv-industria-jocurilor-de-noroc-din-romania-in-date-oficiale-scaderi-majore-in-perioada-2022-2025.html

### 3.11 Greece, Portugal, Belgium, Malta

**Greece (EEEP, 2025)** `[V]` — total GGR **€3.07bn (+6.7%)**; **online €1.19bn (+11.7%) = 38.79%**; online split is fixed-odds betting 40.3% / live casino, poker and slots 59.7%; **24 licensed online operators**; public revenues from gambling €1.17bn (+11.2%). **From 1 July 2026** the tax on player winnings rises: €100–€500 from 15% to 20%; above €500 from 20% to 30%. **Sports betting is excluded** — the government explicitly targeted online casino products.
Sources: https://igamingbusiness.com/finance/greece-saw-modest-revenue-rise-with-land-based-dominance-2025-eeep-reports/ · https://globalgaminginsider.com/news/3251/greek-ggr-rises-67-to-eur307bn-as-online-gambling-drives-growth

**Portugal (SRIJ, 2025)** `[V]` — total online GGR **~€1.21bn (+8.5%)**; **online casino >€770m**; Q4 online GGR €337.6m with casino €214m = 63.4% (+15.9% YoY). **Slot-style machine games = 80.4% of all online casino wagering in Q4.** IEJO Q4 €99.3m (+11.3%).
Sources: https://theigaming.eu/2026/04/22/portugal-online-gambling-hits-e1-21bn-ggr-in-2025/ · https://theigaming.eu/2026/03/17/portugal-q4-2025-online-ggr-rises-4-5-to-e337-6m/

**Belgium** — **11% of GGR**, set regionally, one of Europe's most attractive rates; since 2024 no longer a deductible business expense (Constitutional Court dismissed the challenge on 23 October 2025). **Minimum age 21.** **General advertising ban** unless expressly authorised by Royal Decree; licensees may advertise only on their own site and social accounts, with no interaction, no third-party compensation, and moving-image ads capped at five seconds. **Bonuses banned outright.** Licence numbers capped by law.
Sources: https://iclg.com/practice-areas/gambling-laws-and-regulations/belgium/ · https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/gaming-law-2025/belgium

**Malta (MGA Annual Report 2025)** `[V]` — **302 companies holding 311 licences**; **B2B critical-supply licences 171 versus B2C 131** — B2B outnumbers B2C for the first time, with B2C down from 155 in 2023. **Type 1 (online casino games) = 78.9% of B2C GGR, up 9pp YoY**; sports betting fell to 14.5% from 25.6%. Administrative penalties fell to €162,520 from €306,250.
Source: https://www.mga.org.mt/the-mga-publishes-its-2025-annual-report-and-financial-statements/

The B2B/B2C crossover is the story: point-of-consumption rules destroyed the value of a Maltese B2C licence as a passport, while the 2018 B2B framework made Malta the default establishment jurisdiction for suppliers.

### 3.12 Poland, Finland, Norway, Switzerland, Czechia, Bulgaria, Ukraine

**Poland** — online casino has been a **state monopoly since 2017** (Total Casino). EY analysis via the Warsaw Enterprise Institute: the legal sector holds 70% of the overall online market but only **60% of online casino**; entities registered in tax havens account for **~40% of the online casino market**. WEI 2025 estimate: grey-market turnover **PLN 73.7bn (€17bn)**, >3 million Poles using illegal operators. A rise in the flat winnings tax from 10% to 15% was adopted in October 2025 but **vetoed by the President**. Nothing expected before the 2027 elections.
Sources: https://www.pap.pl/en/news/polands-grey-market-controls-40-pct-online-casino-sector-report-shows · https://igamingbusiness.com/gaming/poland-gambling-tax-casino-liberalisation-reform/

**Finland — Europe's next market opening, 1 July 2027** `[V]` — Gambling Act 10/2026 signed 16 January 2026. Opens betting, **online slot and casino games** and online money bingo to competition; Veikkaus keeps a permanent monopoly on lotteries, scratch cards and all physical slot machines. **Tax 22% of GGR**; five-year licences; annual supervision fees €4,000–€400,000. **B2B track: software suppliers apply for a separate game software licence from 1 July 2027, and from 2028 licensed operators may only use software from Finnish-licensed suppliers** (sources differ on whether the date is 1 January or 1 July 2028). **50 B2C applications by June 2026**, up from 24 in March.

### 4.2 Why iCasino expansion has stalled

Seven live states, one new legalisation in two years.

- **Maryland**: HB 295 and HB 1226 passed the House in March 2026 but **neither cleared the Senate Budget and Taxation Committee** before the 13 April session end. Deferred to 2027.
- **Virginia**: SB 579 (a sweepstakes ban paired with iGaming legalisation projecting **$343m in iGaming tax revenue by FY28**) got a single committee discussion before being punted to 2027.
- **New York**: enacted a sweepstakes ban (S5935A) in December 2025 rather than an iGaming bill, framed by Senator Addabbo as a step toward eventual licensed iGaming.
- **Maine**: the sole 2025 legalisation; the bill did not become law until early 2026.

Structural blockers are land-based cannibalisation fear, casino union opposition and tribal exclusivity. The 2025 data cuts both ways: iGaming **overtaking** land-based casino revenue in NJ and PA is the strongest substitution evidence yet, and is used by both sides.

### 5.2 Colombia — the deposit-tax mistake, and the correction

### 6.2 India — the largest single market closure in modern iGaming

**Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 (Act No. 32 of 2025)** `[V]`
- Presidential assent August 2025; Rules notified 22 April 2026; **both in force 1 May 2026**
- **All "online money games" flatly prohibited** — the Act eliminates the decades-old skill-versus-chance defence. **No licensing pathway, no size exemption, no grandfathering.**
- Scope covers real-money casino games, sports betting, fantasy sports, rummy, poker, crypto-denominated gambling and prediction markets
- Penalties up to **three years' imprisonment** and fines up to ₹1 crore. Foreign operators must geo-block by IP and payment.
- Government justification cited **450 million people affected** and losses exceeding ₹20,000 crore

**Market impact** — Dream11, MPL, Zupee, Games24x7, A23, PokerBaazi and Probo all suspended or discontinued money gaming; Flutter ceased money-based online gaming through Junglee. ICICI Securities: the sector **shrank ~80%, from ~$6bn to ~$1bn**, with an estimated ₹25,000 crore in lost annual tax revenue and significant migration of demand to offshore and crypto-linked platforms. Constitutional challenges are **pending before the Supreme Court**.
Sources: https://www.mondaq.com/india/gaming/1784444/indias-new-online-gaming-framework-analysis-of-the-2025-act-and-2026-rules · https://www.fortuneindia.com/business-news/rmg-ban-shrinks-indias-gaming-industry-from-6-bn-to-1-bn-pubg-free-fire-emerge-as-dominant-players/137169

Note: Ontario's regulator described Arrise Solutions Limited — the Pragmatic Play entity — as "India-based" in its May 2026 enforcement coverage. Arrise has substantial Indian operations, so India's ban is an operational as well as a commercial issue for the group.

### 6.3 Japan — illegal, and being enforced harder

Online casino gambling from within Japan is a **criminal offence under the Penal Code**, even where the operator is licensed offshore. The **revised Basic Act on Gambling Addiction Countermeasures** took effect **25 September 2025**, banning the operation of online casinos including as smartphone apps, banning referral sites, and banning posting information directing users to gambling platforms including via social media.

**Enforcement 2025 (NPA, reported April 2026):** **317 people arrested or subject to enforcement action — a record since tracking began in 2018**, up 38 YoY. Online casino cases **more than tripled to 150**. 25 individuals across 8 cases were operators, affiliates or payment service providers, including four arrested for promoting overseas casinos via video streaming.

**Illegal channel size:** NPA survey estimates **~3.37 million people have used online casinos** (nearly 60% in their 20s and 30s), with **total wagers estimated at ¥1.24 trillion per year**.
Sources: https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2025/09/24/japan/crime-legal/gambling-law-takes-effect/ · https://gamblingtalk.net/news/japan-records-317-online-gambling-enforcement-actions-in-2025-highest-since-2018

### 6.4 South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Thailand, SEA, China

**South Korea** — online gambling prohibited. Only state-run Sports Toto and lotteries, plus Kangwon Land as the sole domestic casino open to Koreans. No reform in progress.

**Australia** `[V on legislation]` — **online casino and online slots are prohibited under the Interactive Gambling Act 2001**; only sports and race wagering and some lotteries are legal online. Online wagering GGR estimated at **$5.2–5.8bn** `[E]`; the offshore market is estimated to cost Australians **~A$4bn a year and growing at 2.5× the rate of the legal market** (Responsible Wagering Australia). The **Interactive Gambling Amendment (Gambling Reform) Bill 2026** bans wagering advertising during live sport, bans online keno and foreign matched lottery products, and strengthens BetStop. A separate **Stop the Gambling Ads Bill 2026** introduced 30 March 2026 would prohibit interactive wagering advertising outright. Government analysis suggests the partial ad ban reduces gambling expenditure by only ~0.8%.
Source: https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7459

**New Zealand — a genuine new market** `[V]` — the Online Casino Gambling Act is in force, with the licensed regime **not expected to be fully operational until 2027**. **Up to 15 licences** will be awarded competitively; each valid up to three years, renewable for five; **one brand per licence, maximum three per entity**. **From 1 December 2026, providers that have not applied must cease operating in New Zealand.**
Source: https://www.dia.govt.nz/Online-Gambling-for-Providers

**Thailand** `[V]` — all gambling illegal except the state lottery and limited licensed horse racing; online gambling fully prohibited. The **Entertainment Complex Bill was withdrawn from the parliamentary agenda on 7–9 July 2025** and remains shelved with no active timeline. The WebD platform **blocked 184,000 gambling URLs between October 2025 and January 2026**.

**Indonesia and Vietnam** — both prohibit online gambling for citizens. Both are large grey-demand markets served entirely by offshore operators. Blask's traffic-derived index puts Indonesia at $3.9bn `[E — traffic proxy, not GGR]`.

**China** — all gambling illegal except state welfare and sports lotteries; cross-border solicitation of Chinese nationals is criminalised. Online casino demand from mainland players is served entirely offshore and represents the single largest unquantifiable grey pool in the world.

## 7. Africa and the Middle East

### 7.1 South Africa — the most consequential regulatory fight in Africa

- **Total gambling GGR R75bn in FY2024/25** `[E]`, with ~49% of all gambling occurring online.
- **Online casino remains illegal** under the National Gambling Act 2004. Only **online sports and horse race betting through provincially licensed bookmakers** is lawful.
- **The casino-content workaround is under attack.** On **21 October 2025 the Supreme Court of Appeal ruled in *Portapa t/a Supabets v Casino Association of South Africa*** that bookmakers licensed under the Gauteng Gambling Act cannot offer fixed-odds bets on casino games including roulette. The Western Cape board says the ruling does not affect its licensees; the NGB argues the implications are national. **Unresolved as of April 2026.**
- **NGB notice to Provincial Licensing Authorities, 25 February 2026** `[V]`: **Remote Gambling Servers do not constitute lawful gambling systems**; RGS **shall not be submitted for certification against SANS 1718**, no Letters of Certification will be issued or renewed, and PLAs are directed to prohibit approval or continued use of RGS. Online gambling lawyers claim the NGB is exceeding its authority.
- The **Remote Gambling Bill B11-2024** is the only legislative route to a regulated online casino market. **No implementation expected before 2027.**

Source: https://www.ngb.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Media-Release_RGS.pdf · https://www.itweb.co.za/article/gambling-boards-bid-to-kill-online-gambling-tech-draws-fire/mYZRXM9gzkgvOgA8

This is directly material for slot suppliers: the RGS directive attempts to make the *infrastructure* that delivers third-party slot content uncertifiable.

### 7.2 Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana

**Nigeria** — the **November 2024 Supreme Court ruling voided the National Lottery Act 2005**, ending federal regulation of gaming outside Abuja. Regulation now belongs to the **36 states individually**, with the **Lagos State Lotteries and Gaming Authority** as the anchor regulator. Indicative Lagos costs: ~₦1m application fee, ₦100m licence fee, ₦50m annual renewal, plus a 2.5% levy on sales revenue. Estimated ~$0.8bn online GGR with 60m+ regular bettors `[E]`.

**Kenya** — the **Gambling Control Act 2025 (Act No. 14 of 2025)** creates the Gambling Control Authority, replacing the BCLB, and for the first time sets out **explicit online licence categories including an online casino licence**. Estimated ~$0.6bn online GGR, M-Pesa-driven `[E]`.
Source: https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2025/14/eng@2025-08-12

**Ghana** — the Gaming Commission licenses online casino and sports betting. **No landmark 2025/2026 reform was found in this research pass**, and no tax or GGR figures are asserted.

### 7.3 UAE — a brand-new regulated market, deliberately tiny

**General Commercial Gaming Regulatory Authority (GCGRA)** `[V]` — established September 2023 by federal decree, headquartered in Abu Dhabi, with **exclusive federal jurisdiction** over lottery, internet gaming, sports wagering and land-based facilities. **Five licence categories** including Internet Gaming Operator. Access is **invitation-to-apply only** and **fees are not published**.

**Licences granted to date:** **Coin Technology Projects LLC** (internet gaming and sports wagering — the only online licensee, restricted to users physically in the UAE), **The Game LLC** (lottery), **Wynn Al Marjan Island** (land-based, opening spring 2027).

Operating or facilitating commercial gaming without a GCGRA licence is illegal, and the prohibition **extends to third parties providing products or services** — i.e. suppliers.
Source: https://www.gcgra.gov.ae/en/licensing/

**Morocco** — MDJS and SOREC operate as state monopolies; online casino is not licensed. No formal reform enacted in 2025 or 2026.

### 8.5 Regulatory actions, enforcement and criticism

**1. AGCO (Ontario), 7 May 2026 — CAD $40,000 Order of Monetary Penalty.** `[V — primary source]`
The Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario served **Arrise Solutions Limited** (trading as Pragmatic Play) and **Relax Gaming Limited** with Orders of Monetary Penalties of **$40,000 each**, after finding games created by both companies were **available on unregulated gambling websites accessible to Ontario players**. Both cooperated fully and took prompt action to restrict Ontario player access. Dr. Karin Schnarr, AGCO CEO and Registrar: *"When regulated games appear on unregulated sites, it risks enabling a market that exposes players to real harm."*
Sources: https://www.agco.ca/en/news/agco-takes-enforcement-action-against-two-companies-allowing-their-games-unregulated-gaming · https://www.ctvnews.ca/toronto/article/ontario-regulator-fines-2-gaming-companies-40k-each-after-their-games-found-on-unregulated-gambling-websites/

**This is the only confirmed regulatory monetary penalty against Pragmatic Play that this research identified.**

**2. Los Angeles City Attorney civil enforcement action, August 2025.** The City of Los Angeles filed a civil enforcement action against **Stake.us** alleging illegal gambling, **naming several suppliers including Pragmatic Play**, alongside Evolution, Hacksaw Gaming, Nolimit City, Red Tiger, Big Time Gaming and NetEnt.
Source: https://next.io/news/casino/evolution-pragmatic-exit-stake-us-california/

**3. US sweepstakes exit, 2 September 2025.** `[V — company statement]` Pragmatic Play became **the first major supplier to withdraw from the US sweepstakes vertical**: *"Pragmatic Play has chosen to discontinue licensing its games to sweepstake operators in US States where restrictions were not already in place, in light of regulatory developments and evolving legislation."* On Stake.us the games were **locked rather than removed**, which trade coverage read as leaving the door open.
Source: https://next.io/news/regulation/exclusive-pragmatic-play-ends-supply-sweepstakes/

**4. Netherlands geo-blocking, 2026.** Pragmatic Play began **blocking Dutch IP addresses** on unlicensed casinos. Blask quantified the impact: 25 of the top 86 slots and 28 of the top 100 games on Dutch offshore lobbies were Pragmatic Play titles — **roughly 29% of the top slot inventory removed in one move.** Neither Pragmatic Play nor the Ksa responded to requests for comment, so it is not established whether this was voluntary, regulator-driven or a risk reassessment. **No KSA fine against Pragmatic Play was found.**
Source: https://blask.com/newsroom/pragmatic-play-netherlands-offshore-slots/

**5. No UKGC enforcement action against Pragmatic Play was found.** The relevant comparator is Evolution. `[V]`

> The UKGC opened a licence review of **Evolution Malta Holding Limited** in December 2024 after identifying **five Evolution games on six unlicensed websites** with large volumes of UK visits between December 2023 and November 2024. The review concluded in July 2026 with a **£4.75m settlement**. The Commission found Evolution's money-laundering risk assessment failed to flag that two operator customers were supplying its games to Great Britain without a Commission licence, breaching Licence Conditions 12.1.1 and 12.1.2. John Pierce, Director of Enforcement: *"The Commission's investigation and testing uncovered failings that were serious enough for us to consider licence suspension."* The settlement adds a new licence condition requiring an independent audit within 12 months.
> Sources: https://sbcnews.co.uk/europe/uk/2026/07/23/evolution-uk-licence/ · https://fortunaria.com/news/ukgc-details-aml-third-party-failures-behind-evolution-settlement

Pragmatic Play holds a UK licence and its content is far more widely distributed on offshore sites than Evolution's. The Evolution settlement is the clearest available read on Pragmatic Play's UK regulatory risk profile.

**6. Pragmatic Play as plaintiff.** Pragmatic Play is suing the "El Dorado Network" in the **US District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia** over a network of roughly 200 counterfeit sites replicating its games.

**7. Russia exposure — not established.** Searched, not found, not asserted.

### 8.6 Reading of Pragmatic Play's position

Commercially: the strongest slot content share on earth across Europe, LATAM and Africa, and effectively zero presence in the world's largest and fastest-growing regulated online casino market. Structurally: a distribution model — maximum reach through aggregators and offshore operators — that is precisely the model three regulators (AGCO, UKGC, Ksa) and two legislatures (Romania, Finland) have moved to make expensive in the last eighteen months. The sweepstakes exit, the Dutch geo-block and the reported US ambitions all point the same direction: cleaning up distribution to buy a US licence. Whether it succeeds is the single most consequential open question in the slot supply market.
