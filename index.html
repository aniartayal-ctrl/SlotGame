02 · Where Pragmatic is

Broadly global.
Unevenly proven.

Pragmatic Play says its content is certified or licensed in 30+ jurisdictions. Strong regulator and operator evidence exists across Europe, Canada, Latin America and Africa. Public evidence is notably thinner in regulated U.S. iCasino.

Core strength · strongest independent evidence

Europe

  • 17.8% YTD GGR share in Eilers-Fantini's May 2026 sample
  • UKGC licence 56015; MGA B2B licence under Arrise Solutions
  • Named launches in UK, Spain, Sweden, Germany, Italy, Greece, Denmark and Czechia
Strong · concentrated

Canada

  • Ontario launches with bet365 and NorthStar
  • 18.6% supplier GGR share reported in Dec 2024 sample
  • Alberta certified portfolio announced at July 2026 opening
Expansion engine

Latin America

  • Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Paraguay and provincial Argentina evidence
  • Smart Studio localisation is a differentiator
  • Brazil certification is title/operator-specific; no unified public game list
Selective reach · mixed legality

Africa & Asia

  • Operator/platform deals in South Africa, Ghana, Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda
  • PhilWeb Philippine rollout targeted Q3 2026—not fully live at cutoff
  • Mainland China, India, Japan and Singapore sharply restrict online casino access
Blind spot: Canada is not a proxy for the U.S. No robust public launch was found in the seven regulated U.S. full-iCasino states; Light & Wonder, IGT and local/omnichannel suppliers have a structural lead there.

05 · Blind spots

Where the
machine stalls.

Pragmatic's broad distribution can hide concentrated risks: franchise sameness, a weak U.S. public footprint, category leaders it cannot dislodge, and limited disclosure beyond slots.

Priority 01 · High

U.S. regulated iCasino

Only seven states offered full online casino in 2025, but they generated $10.74bn and grew 27.6%. Public Pragmatic launch evidence is weak; L&W and IGT own omnichannel familiarity.

→
Move

Secure an anchor state/operator and build U.S.-specific cabinet-to-mobile or local-IP proof.

Priority 02 · High

Franchise fatigue

“1000”, “Super Scatter”, Megaways and endless Big Bass extensions monetize recognition but reduce creative surprise and increase dependency on a few IP families.

→
Move

Ring-fence experimental studios and track net-new-IP retention separately from sequel output.

Priority 03 · Medium

Crash is already owned

Spaceman is visible, but Aviator's claimed scale and consistent market leadership create a social/network moat that feature parity will not break.

→
Move

Differentiate around teams, tournaments and creator/community formats—not a reskinned multiplier curve.

Priority 04 · Medium

Regional game language

Global hits do not erase local taste. CEE favors familiar fruit/book/link games; LatAm rewards local IP; Asian-origin suppliers lead fish, portrait and mobile-social formats.

→
Move

Give local studios product authority, not just translation and themed skins.

Priority 05 · Medium

Live remains #2

Pragmatic's 800+ bespoke tables are formidable, but Evolution's ~2,000 tables, 24 studios and game-show IP remain the deeper production moat.

→
Move

Win with Smart Studio localisation and slot-to-live IP speed instead of matching infrastructure table-for-table.

Priority 06 · Foundation

Disclosure & diligence

“30+ jurisdictions” does not identify entities, verticals, licence numbers or expiry dates. Private ownership also obscures revenue mix and customer concentration.

→
Move

Publish a market/entity/product licence matrix and consistent portfolio/performance KPIs.
